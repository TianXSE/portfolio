To me, physics is the part of science that feels most like art. Art lets us appreciate the universe through what we see and hear; physics explains the rules underneath that make it possible.

It doesn't change what we see. It changes how we look.

My interest began around high school, maybe a little earlier. I already had a solid base in math, and classmates often turned to me when formulas appeared. Then I read about people like Einstein and got hooked on how strangely well mathematics describes reality.

{% endcall %} {% call card("math", "Math") %}

People around me say I'm good with numbers, and I see why. But being called good at something doesn't stop you from comparing yourself, and meeting real prodigies has made me question my own level more than once.

I can't say when I started loving math, or whether I ever loved numbers themselves. One of my earliest memories is learning the abacus at around four or five, multiplying numbers into the hundreds and thousands. I didn't think much of it then, but that may be where it began.

I'm not someone who grinds eighteen hours a day to prove a point, and I'm still learning to be consistent with what I care about. What I do enjoy is the mix of logic, numbers and abstraction, and the moment a problem that looked impossible starts to make sense. Maybe that matters more to me than being the best.
